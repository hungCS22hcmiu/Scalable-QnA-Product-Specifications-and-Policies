### 2026-09-06  ·  ~3 h  ·  Three measurements: one hypothesis refuted, one confirmed, one rule beaten

**Did:** Followed an objection the author raised — *"why doesn't the request say which product is
being asked about? a real store has many laptops with different spec docs"* — through prior-art
search, three measurements, and a working implementation. The objection is the sharpest one
recorded against C1, and ADR-028 answers it by **rejecting `product_id` on the grounds that it
"forces C1's claim to be restated as materially weaker."** That is reasoning from the desired
conclusion, and the ADR concedes in the same line that the rejected option is *"more faithful to
production traffic."* The measurements below were run to settle it with data instead.

**Prior art found first, because it changes what can be claimed.**

- **arXiv 2510.26835, *Category-Aware Semantic Caching for Heterogeneous LLM Workloads***
  (Oct 2025) — per-category thresholds, TTLs and quotas, categories from a classifier. Reports
  40-60 % hit for high-repetition categories against 5-15 % for low. This is a **second ADR-026
  moment**: per-category cache policy is now concurrent work and **cannot be claimed as novel**.
- **arXiv 2508.01990, Flipkart's production PQA assistant** (5 M MAU) — a BERT intent model with
  `product_spec` (F1 89.38), `return_policy` (**99.46**), `warranty` (95.63) as separate classes,
  routing retrieval per intent, 93.17 % top-1 overall. **No caching.** So the spec/policy boundary
  is real, load-bearing in production, and easy to classify — and nobody has connected it to a
  cache.
- **Production semantic-cache practice** — namespace scoping per tenant is the default (*"every
  cache entry is scoped to the calling user"*), real hit rates 20-45 %, and 2-3 categories carry
  60-70 % of traffic. The author's `product_id` instinct **is** what production does.

**Measurement 1 — read-side channel decomposition. REFUTED.**

Hypothesis: splitting containment into a product channel and a policy channel would separate
cases the pooled score cannot. Ten hand-built cases with ground truth read off the policy texts:

| Group | Truth | pooled | product ch. | policy ch. |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A cross-category return (30d vs 14d+15%) | REFUSE | 0.60 · 0.60 · 0.60 | **0.00 · 0.00 · 0.00** | 1.00 · 0.75 · 1.00 |
| B same policy, different product (both 30d) | **REUSE** | 0.60 · 0.40 · 0.80 | **0.00 · 0.00 · 0.00** | 1.00 · 1.00 · 1.00 |
| C same product, different policy (30d vs 1yr) | REFUSE | 0.60 · 0.60 | 0.50 · 1.00 | **0.67 · 0.50** |
| D warranty across products (1yr vs 2yr) | REFUSE | 0.40 · 0.60 | 0.00 · 0.00 | 1.00 · 0.75 |

The product channel reads **0.00 for both A and B**, so it cannot tell a correct refusal from a
correct reuse — it behaves exactly like a `product_id` cache key, refusing the safe reuse too. The
policy channel reads ~1.00 for both. **Neither channel separates A from B.** The earlier claim in
this log's own reasoning — *"policy determines the answer, product is context"* — is wrong, and
the data says so.

**⚠️ Root cause, and it is the finding worth keeping: `dev-v0` holds 4 policy chunks and `top_k`
is 5.** The top-5 can contain the entire policy space on every query, so containment over the
policy channel is ≈1 **by construction**, not by meaning. A new corpus constraint follows that no
ADR states:

> The policy channel carries information only when `|policy chunks| >> top_k`.

ADR-024 requires ">=3 chunks per policy document", which at 4 policy documents gives 12 chunks —
2.4x `top_k`, still far too few. The constraint needed is more policy **documents** (category x
condition x seller tier), not just more chunks per document. This also explains the W05/Day-0
finding that similarity sits flat at 0.848-0.865 regardless of product: with 4 policies every
policy question retrieves the same evidence, so **similarity and overlap are uninformative for
the same reason**. Two symptoms, one cause, now quantified.

**Measurement 2 — write-side kind-mix. CONFIRMED, and it survives the saturation above.**

`policy_frac` of a single entry's own grounding, 6 spec questions against 6 policy questions:

| | policy_frac |
| :--- | :--- |
| spec ("battery life of the EarBuds Pop 3", "RAM of the UltraBook Pro 14", …) | **0.00 — all six** |
| policy ("return window", "warranty", "restocking fee", …) | **0.40 - 0.80** |

Total separation, gap 0.40, no overlap. It survives what killed measurement 1 because it is a
property of **one** set's composition, never a comparison between two: saturation makes every
policy question retrieve the same policies, but a spec question still retrieves **zero** of them.
Mechanism, not coincidence — spec text is lexically far from policy text.

For scale: Flipkart needs a BERT intent model at 93.17 % top-1 to draw this line. Here it is a
prefix count with no model and **no error term** — which matters because a classifier error would
be a false-hit cause with no bucket in `experiment-protocol.md` §4's two-cause split, charged to
the reuse rule. That is exactly why ADR-018 dropped the bypass classifier.

**Measurement 3 — namespace-from-grounding against containment, head to head.**

Implemented and ran it (see *Shipped*). One rule: `sim >= tau AND namespace(q) == namespace(e)`;
only the **source** of the namespace differs by lane — POLICY takes the rank-1 policy chunk's
`doc_id`, SPEC takes `product_id` from the request, falling back to the rank-1 product doc.

| Set | tau | Namespace rule | Containment rule |
| :--- | :-- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demo set**, steps 3 + 4 | 0.85 | 2/2 correct | 2/2 correct — **tie** |
| **Policy set**, 4 banded decisions | 0.70 | **4/4** | **2/4** — false hits on sofa-return and on warranty-same-product |
| **Policy set** | 0.85 | 0 hits | 0 hits — never entered the band |

At tau = 0.85 containment is not safe, it is **inert**: nothing reaches it, and the price is that
the capacity case (laptop-return reusing the headphones answer, both 30 days) is refused too. The
namespace rule is correct at tau = 0.70, where containment produces two false hits. **The gain is
that it permits a lower tau safely** — which is a frontier argument, not a point argument.

**The demo is not broken.** Both rules give identical verdicts on all four demo steps; step 4 still
refuses, now because `policy-returns-furniture != policy-returns-electronics`.

**Grounding-derived namespace grouping**, separately: lane classification **10/10**; policy
namespace **7/8**; spec namespace **failed** to unify two paraphrases about one product.

⚠️ **Both failures matter more than the successes.**

1. *"Can I get a full refund on my bookshelf after delivery?"* took rank-1 policy
   `policy-returns-electronics` — wrong, a bookshelf is furniture. Diagnosed: the phrase **"full
   refund" appears verbatim in the electronics policy and nowhere in the furniture policy**, which
   says "14 days, subject to a 15% restocking fee". The embedding matched the policy's *wording*,
   not the product's *category*. **Namespace equality is more discriminating but more brittle:
   one mis-ranked chunk flips the decision, where containment averages over five and moves 0.2.**
   That is the strongest argument for containment and it is not yet quantified — the frequency of
   a wrong rank-1 is the most important measurement still missing.
